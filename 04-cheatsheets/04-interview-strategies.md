# Interview Strategies & Soft Skills

The technical skills get you through, but **soft skills** get you the offer.

---

## Before the Interview

- **Research the company.** Their products, recent news, tech stack, culture.
- **Re-read the job description.** Note key skills mentioned.
- **Practice explaining past projects.** Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
- **Get a good night's sleep.** Sleep > cramming the night before.
- **Set up your environment** (for online): quiet room, stable internet, water.

---

## During a Coding Interview

### 1. Listen & Clarify (1-3 min)
- Repeat the problem in your own words.
- Ask clarifying questions: input size, edge cases, constraints.
- Confirm expected output.

### 2. Brute Force First (1-3 min)
- State the naive approach.
- Mention its complexity.
- This shows problem-solving even if you can't optimize.

### 3. Optimize (5-10 min)
- Think out loud.
- Walk through examples.
- Identify patterns.
- Discuss trade-offs.

### 4. Code (10-15 min)
- Use clear variable names.
- Modular code (helper functions).
- Handle edge cases (write tests mentally).
- Don't go silent — narrate what you're writing.

### 5. Test & Verify (3-5 min)
- Walk through your code with an example.
- Identify bugs out loud.
- Discuss time & space complexity.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

 **Jumping into code without thinking.** Always plan first.

 **Going silent.** Always narrate.

 **Arguing with the interviewer.** They have hints — listen to them.

 **Being arrogant.** "This is trivial." Even if it is, show humility.

 **Not asking questions.** Clarify before you code.

 **Skipping edge cases.** Empty input, single element, duplicates.

 **Bad variable names.** `int x = ...` for important logic → use clear names.

 **Saying "I'm done" without testing.** Always trace through.

## During a System Design Interview

### 1. Clarify Scope (5 min)
- What's in / out of scope?
- Who are the users? DAU?
- Key features (top 3-5)?
- Read-heavy or write-heavy?

### 2. Back-of-Envelope Estimates (3 min)
- QPS, storage, bandwidth.
- Show you can reason about scale.

### 3. High-Level Design (10 min)
- Boxes & arrows.
- Don't dive into details yet.

### 4. Database Schema (5 min)
- For the core entities.
- SQL vs NoSQL — justify your choice.

### 5. Deep Dive (15 min)
- Pick 2-3 critical paths.
- Discuss scaling, caching, consistency.

### 6. Bottlenecks & Trade-offs (5 min)
- What's the weakest link?
- How would you handle 10x scale?
- Trade-offs you made.

## Asking Good Questions (At the End)

Almost every interview ends with "Do you have any questions?" — always ask 1-2.

Good questions:
- "What does a typical day look like for this role?"
- "What's the team's biggest technical challenge right now?"
- "How is success measured in this role?"
- "What's the most exciting project the team has shipped recently?"
- "How does the team handle technical disagreements?"
- "What does career growth look like?"

Bad questions (avoid):
- "What's the salary?" (wait for HR)
- "How much PTO?" (wait for HR)
- Anything easily Google-able

## Behavioral Questions (STAR Method)

**S — Situation:** Set the scene.
**T — Task:** What was your responsibility?
**A — Action:** What did YOU do? (Use "I", not "we")
**R — Result:** Quantify the outcome.

### Common Questions
- "Tell me about a challenging project."
- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate."
- "Tell me about a failure and what you learned."
- "How do you handle tight deadlines?"
- "Describe a time you went above and beyond."

## Salary Negotiation

1. **Never give your number first.** "I'd love to learn more about the role's compensation."
2. **Get the offer first**, then negotiate.
3. **Anchor high.** They expect you to negotiate.
4. **Be polite but firm.** "I'm excited about the role. Based on my research and experience, I was hoping for $X."
5. **Consider total comp.** Base, bonus, equity, benefits, PTO.
6. **Multiple offers = leverage.** Mention politely: "I'm also considering another opportunity."
7. **Walk-away point.** Know your minimum and stick to it.

## Common Phrases That Help

 "Let me think about this for a moment..."
 "What if the input is empty?"
 "I think we can use a hash map here because..."
 "Let me trace through this with an example."
 "Is there a way to optimize the space?"
 "Let me reconsider..."
 "That's a great hint. So we're looking for..."

---

## Mindset

- Interviews are conversations, not tests.
- Interviewers WANT you to succeed.
- One bad interview ≠ bad engineer.
- "No" is data; refine and try again.
- Confidence + humility + clarity = winning formula.
